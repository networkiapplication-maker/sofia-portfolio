OVERVIEW

This case study analyses H&M's customer experience by mapping the journey from awareness to loyalty, identifying pain points and potential solutions, and evaluating brand positioning against key fashion competitors.

01

Awareness

Pain points: Greenwashing doubts and negative comments/reviews.

Opportunity: clearer sustainability claims, faster social replies and supplier transparency.
02

Consideration

Pain points: Size confusion and inconsistent staff support.

Opportunity: better size guides and staff training.
03

Decision

Pain points: Long queues, technical issues and online cart abandonment.

Opportunity: faster checkout/self-checkout and clearer online product information.
04

Purchase

Pain points: Refund delays, wrong/missing items and poor customer service.

Opportunity: improved ERP systems, AI-supported service and stronger review responses.
05

Loyalty

Pain points: Generic emails and weak rewards.

Opportunity: personalised offers, clearer reward tiers and AI-driven customer insights.
POSITIONING ANALYSIS

H&M in the competitive fashion landscape

The positioning map compares H&M with Zara, Uniqlo, Primark and Shein across price and perceived quality, helping identify how H&M can strengthen its customer value proposition.

UNIVERSITY ASSIGNMENT / CASE STUDY

Etihad Airways Marketing Insights

Quantitative research · SPSS · European consumers

17 / 20
MY CONTRIBUTION

My contribution focused mainly on quantitative data analysis and interpretation using IBM SPSS Statistics. I applied descriptive statistics, independent-samples t-tests, regression and chi-square analysis to examine European consumers’ perceptions of Etihad Airways, then developed recommendations around promotional visibility, digital engagement and loyalty.

17/20Assessment
4Analysis methods
SPSSQuantitative analysis
EuropeanConsumer sample
01

Demographic & Descriptive Analysis

Most participants resided in the UAE and had European origins. Ages ranged from 18–25, with gender distribution at 50% apart from one unspecified response. 54.2% were aware of Etihad, while 27.1% had previously flown with the airline. Awareness averaged 3.35/5.

02

Independent-Samples T-Test

Tested whether promotional awareness differed between respondents who had flown with Etihad and those who had not. The difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.663).

03

Regression Analysis

Examined whether service perceptions influenced awareness of Etihad’s promotional campaigns. The overall model was not statistically significant (F = 0.744, p = 0.655), with all service-attribute coefficients above 0.05.

04

Chi-Square Analysis

Found a statistically significant association between traveler type and trip purpose (p = 0.017), and between household classification and air-travel frequency (p = 0.001).

SPSS OUTPUT

Statistical analysis & interpretation

The analysis combined cross-tabulation, significance testing, regression outputs and descriptive statistics. The results were interpreted rather than presented as raw statistical tables alone.

FREQUENCIES & CONSUMER INFLUENCE
44.4%Most common ranking: Emirates → Qatar Airways → Etihad for awareness of discounts/offers.
2.42Mean influence of family recommendations on airline choice.
2.61Mean influence of friends on airline choice.
3.17Mean influence of celebrities/influencers — the weakest listed influence.
FINDINGS & RECOMMENDATIONS

The analysis identified limited promotional visibility, moderate awareness and low usage as key challenges in the European sample. Recommendations focused on frequent digital campaigns around discounts and loyalty programmes, seasonal promotions aligned with leisure and educational travel, and referral or family-oriented rewards that leverage the stronger influence of close personal networks.
